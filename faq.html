About the Exams

  • TEF (Test d'Évaluation de Français) and TCF (Test de Connaissance du Français) both measure French proficiency, but they have different formats and are accepted in different contexts. TEF Canada is widely used for Canadian immigration through Express Entry and uses the NCLC scoring scale. TCF Canada is also accepted for permanent residence and uses CLB equivalencies. Both have four components — reading, listening, writing, and speaking — but the question styles, timing, and scoring methods differ. We help you identify which exam fits your specific immigration or study goal, then prepare you accordingly.
  • For most Express Entry streams with French as a second language, you typically need CLB 7 (TCF) or NCLC 7 (TEF) in all four components. Some provincial nominee programmes have different thresholds. For bilingual draws (which can significantly boost your CRS score), higher scores in French alongside English language proficiency are required. We advise on the exact score target based on your profile during your placement session.
  • DELF (Diplôme d'Études en Langue Française) is an internationally recognised French language diploma covering levels A1 through B2. DALF (Diplôme Approfondi de Langue Française) covers C1 and C2 — advanced and mastery levels. Both are issued by the French Ministry of Education and are valid for life. They are commonly required for university admission in French-speaking countries, immigration to France, and professional accreditation. We most commonly prepare students for DELF B1, DELF B2, and DALF C1.
  • For students who are already at or near the target level: 2–3 months of focused exam technique work is usually sufficient. For students who need to build both language proficiency and exam skills: typically 4–6 months. We give you an honest timeline after we understand your starting level and your deadline. We never rush students toward an exam they are not ready for.
  • It depends on your goal and test centre availability in your city. For most Canadian immigration applicants, either TEF Canada or TCF Canada is accepted. The key differences: TEF tends to use more formal, structured question formats; TCF is often described as more natural and conversational. Some students find one format suits their learning style better. We can walk you through both in your first session and help you choose based on your situation.
