Common questions

Quick answers.

  • TEF (Test d'Évaluation de Français) and TCF (Test de Connaissance du Français) are both French proficiency exams, but they serve slightly different purposes. TEF is commonly used for Canadian immigration via Express Entry, targeting NCLC scores. TCF is accepted for Canadian permanent residence (TCF Canada) and is also used for French citizenship applications. Both test reading, listening, writing, and speaking — but the question formats and scoring systems differ. We help you choose the right exam for your goal, then prepare you to ace it.
  • Absolutely. We welcome learners from A1 all the way through B2 and beyond. Your first session includes a short, informal placement check so we know exactly where to start — no pressure, no test stress. Many of our students began with zero French and have gone on to pass DELF B2 and TEF exam targets.
  • Large classes hide weak spots. When 30 students share one tutor, you can sit quietly for an hour and leave with the same mistakes you came in with. We limit every session by design — private classes for deep, personalised work, and mini-groups (3–5 students) for collaborative practice at an accessible price. Either way, you are corrected, challenged, and actually heard in every session.
  • Most students reach their target score in 3–6 months of consistent preparation. The exact timeline depends on your starting level, your target CLB or NCLC score, and how much time you can commit per week. Students who join at B1 with a CLB 7 target typically need 3–4 months. Those starting from A2 usually need 5–7 months. We will give you an honest estimate after your placement session.
